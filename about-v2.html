The story

It only looks like a straight line in hindsight.

I started making things for the web in Brazil in the 1990s. I didn’t think of it as a career. It was just the most interesting thing in the room.

Written down, what came after looks clean. Brazil, California, Amsterdam, Canada, Australia. Album Agency, Booking.com, Shopify, Atlassian, Xero, Figma. Six lines, three decades, one direction. It reads like a plan.

It wasn’t one. There were visas, and the waiting that comes with them. Countries where I didn’t speak the language yet. Titles that changed while the work didn’t, and work that changed while the title didn’t. A family that moved every time I did. Most of it got decided with incomplete information and a deadline — which is also a fair description of design.

Booking.com taught me to put the customer at the centre and actually mean it: test it, measure it, let the answer be no. Atlassian said the same thing with the politeness removed — don’t #@!% the customer — and I haven’t found a better version of it since.

What carried across all of it is a stubbornness about the whole product. The question nobody asked. The system underneath. How the team decided. And then the interaction, the typography, the spacing, the pixels. Those aren’t separate concerns to me; they’re the same concern at different distances. A screen is usually wrong for a reason that happened much earlier.

Speaking, writing, teaching, mentoring, interviewing — same curiosity, pointed outward. I made Latinxs Who Design because the conversations I wanted to hear weren’t being recorded, and because a lot of people doing excellent work were doing it quietly.

I live in Sydney. I’m a dad of three, which has clarified my sense of priority more than any framework has.

This page exists partly to keep the messy version visible. The route below is the record. This part is the rest of it.
